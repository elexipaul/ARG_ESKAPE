**Methodology: machine-learning classification of ARG families and drug classes, with protein-annotation interpretation**

*Methods draft prepared on 25 September 2026 from the executed paired-classifier workflow and protein-annotation analysis. Notebook source code, saved configurations, and output tables provide the study-specific evidence. Author verification is pending; this document does not report a new analysis or model run.*

**1. Study design and relationship to the reference studies.** A protein-level computational framework was used to predict antimicrobial resistance gene (ARG) family and drug-class memberships and to investigate the biological context of informative sequence descriptors. The workflow comprised physicochemical feature extraction, supervised classification, held-out SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), comparison of family and drug-class importance profiles, protein annotation, descriptor–annotation association testing, and matched comparisons of alternative predictor sets. The use of test-set SHAP values and mean absolute attribution for global interpretation follows the explanatory approach described by [Cen et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bsheal.2025.11.001). Representation of domains as presence and count matrices, comparison across feature representations, and shuffled-label controls are conceptually related to [Ghosh et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.07.03.663053). These ideas were adapted to individual CARD proteins: the outcomes were database-assigned family and drug-class memberships. Laboratory resistance/susceptibility phenotypes were not the response variables in this analysis.

**2. Protein dataset and outcome annotation.** Protein sequences were obtained from the local CARD protein-homolog-model FASTA, and family, drug-class, and resistance-mechanism memberships were obtained from `aro_index.tsv`. Antibiotic Resistance Ontology (ARO) accessions were used to reconcile sequence records, feature tables, and annotations. Pfeature and ProFET-style tables were joined using drug-class and sequence identifiers, with one-to-one key validation and checks for unmatched rows. Repeated drug-class assignments were collapsed to one row per ARO after confirming identical descriptor values across copies. Protein sequence hashes were retained for identity checks and fold construction. The reconciled dataset contained 6,059 ARO proteins, representing 396 family labels and 39 drug-class labels before modelling filters. Multiple memberships were retained as binary labels rather than forcing each protein into one mutually exclusive category. For each target, a positive label denoted its presence in the CARD record; a negative label denoted absence of that membership in the local index.

**3. Physicochemical feature extraction and preprocessing.** Each protein was represented by 996 numerical descriptors: 956 Pfeature descriptors and 40 features generated by the project's ProFET-style Biopython implementation. Pfeature-derived variables represented amino-acid and dipeptide composition, physicochemical group composition, atomic and bond composition, residue-distribution and entropy measures, conjoint triads, and pseudo-composition and sequence-order descriptors. The supplementary implementation calculated sequence length, molecular weight, isoelectric point, aromaticity, instability index, grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY), net charge at pH 5, 7, and 9, and aliphatic index. These ten measurements were calculated for the full protein and for its N-terminal, middle, and C-terminal thirds. Terminal thirds were sequence partitions, not experimentally defined domains. The extraction approach used [Pfeature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36251780/); the additional 40 descriptors should be described as a custom ProFET-style implementation rather than as execution of the complete ProFET package.

Columns beginning with `PRI_` or `RRI_` were excluded according to the implemented feature schema. Infinite values were converted to missing values, and consistency of the retained 996-column schema was checked. The paired physicochemical classifier passed missing values directly to LightGBM. Median imputation and standardization used to construct approximate descriptor groups were separate from the predictive input matrix. In the later annotation ablations, constant or entirely unavailable predictors were removed within each training partition, and median imputation was fitted on that partition only.

**4. Family and drug-class classifier development.** Independent one-versus-rest binary classifiers were fitted for supported ARG families and drug classes using [LightGBM](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/6449f44a102fde848669bdd9eb6b76fa-Abstract.html). This binary-relevance design allowed simultaneous predictions for multiple families or drug classes. Targets required at least ten positive and ten negative proteins, at least two contributing groups in each state, and both states in the retained outer training and test partitions. Following these checks, the paired SHAP workflow evaluated 84 family and 27 drug-class targets.

Models used a binary objective, 300 boosting estimators, 15 leaves, a learning rate of 0.05, a minimum of ten observations per leaf, and an L2 regularization parameter of 1.0. Class imbalance was handled with `is_unbalance=True`. Random seed 0, deterministic mode, and column-wise computation were specified. The model configuration used two CPU threads. These were fixed hyperparameters; the executed paired workflow did not conduct a hyperparameter search.

**5. Validation and decision thresholds for the SHAP classifiers.** The original paired-classifier analysis used approximate descriptor-based grouping because an external sequence-cluster file was not supplied. A radius-neighbour graph was constructed in standardized, median-imputed descriptor space with radius 1.0, and connected components defined groups. Exact sequence-hash matches were also linked. This procedure produced 6,045 groups among 6,059 proteins. A shared five-fold stratified group cross-validation scheme was used for family and drug-class models, with groups kept within a single fold and checks that both target states occurred in training and test partitions. Descriptor proximity was an approximate grouping criterion and did not establish sequence-homology separation.

Within each outer training set, up to three grouped inner folds were used to select the classification threshold by maximizing F1 over 0.05–0.95 in increments of 0.01. Ties favoured the largest threshold. Where an inner split containing both target states could not be constructed, the threshold defaulted to 0.50 and the fallback was recorded. Each outer test protein was scored by a model that had not trained on it. Performance was summarized using average precision (AP), area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUROC), F1, precision, recall, accuracy, and confusion matrices. Multilabel summaries included micro- and macro-averaged AP and F1, exact-match accuracy, and Hamming loss. ROC and precision–recall curves were generated from the stored out-of-fold scores. These estimates were reported separately from the later MMseqs2-grouped ablations.

**6. SHAP estimation and comparison of family–drug-class profiles.** Feature contributions were calculated for outer-held-out proteins using [SHAP TreeExplainer](https://shap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/shap.TreeExplainer.html), with tree-path-dependent perturbation and raw model output. Attribution values therefore described contributions to the model's raw binary decision margin. Additivity was checked by comparing the base value plus summed feature attributions with the model's raw score. For target \(c\) and feature \(j\), global importance was defined as

\[
I_{cj}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\phi_{icj}|,
\]

where each protein contributed an attribution from its outer test fold. Fold summaries were weighted by the number of explained proteins, yielding one complete importance profile per target. These mean absolute values quantified importance without specifying whether a larger descriptor value increased or decreased a target's score.

A study-specific profile filter retained targets satisfying \(\max_j I_{cj}\leq2.0\). Thus, the implemented rule applied to the largest mean absolute importance within a target profile, rather than to individual protein-level SHAP values or to isolated features. It was an interpretive filter, not a generally validated mathematical safety criterion. Up to 15 features with nonzero importance were retained per target. For each family–drug-class pair passing the filter, overlap between the two selected feature sets was measured by Jaccard similarity,

\[
J(F,D)=\frac{|F\cap D|}{|F\cup D|},
\]

and Spearman correlation was calculated between the complete, aligned 996-feature importance profiles. Pairs were classified as CARD-linked when the family and drug class co-occurred in a record in the local ARO index. Linked and unlinked pairs were ranked separately by decreasing Jaccard similarity and then Spearman correlation. Model quality flags identified targets with AP or F1 below 0.50. A CARD-unlinked pair was treated as an unverified association candidate, not automatically as a false-positive biological relationship.

**7. Model storage and query-sequence classification.** Following evaluation, the binary classifiers were refitted on the complete dataset and stored together in a single model archive containing native LightGBM models, target labels, feature order, decision thresholds, software metadata, and applicability references. The archive was one deployable bundle containing multiple binary models. Deployment thresholds were selected from pooled out-of-fold scores; reported validation F1 used the separately selected inner-fold thresholds. Model serialization was checked by comparing predictions before and after reloading.

The inference workflow extracts the same physicochemical descriptors from a query protein, checks feature compatibility, and scores its family and drug-class memberships. A training-fitted applicability heuristic uses median imputation, robust scaling, 50 principal components, nearest-neighbour distance, and reconstruction residuals, with cutoffs based on the 99th percentiles of training-reference distributions. The workflow also checks sequence identity against training hashes. Applicability and model-quality flags qualify predictions; classifier scores are uncalibrated membership scores. This inference procedure does not itself establish experimental resistance or demonstrate performance on genuinely novel ARG families.

**8. InterProScan annotation and construction of protein-by-annotation matrices.** All 6,059 cohort proteins were analysed with [InterProScan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3998142/) version 5.78-109.0. The configured launcher used Java 11, batches of 250 proteins, and 12 CPU threads, producing XML and TSV outputs. The recorded applications and library releases were Pfam 38.2, PROSITE Patterns 2026_01, PROSITE Profiles 2026_01, Gene3D 4.3.0, and SUPERFAMILY 1.75. InterPro lookup, Gene Ontology mapping, and pathway annotation were requested; the launcher disabled the precomputed match lookup service. XML records were used to verify cohort scan coverage, including proteins without reported matches.

Raw annotations retained protein identifiers, signature accessions and names, source libraries, integrated InterPro entries, match coordinates, scores, E-values, and available hidden Markov model (HMM) coordinates. Pfam and integrated InterPro annotations were encoded as separate binary presence matrices with one ARO protein per row. Pfam copy counts were calculated after deduplicating repeated accession-and-coordinate matches. Quantitative Pfam variables included match score, score divided by HMM length, matched-region length divided by protein length, and matched HMM span divided by HMM length. For repeated hits, the maximum available value for each quantitative field was retained per protein–domain pair. These coverage values represented individual matched regions rather than union coverage across all domain copies. Non-hit quantitative scores remained missing. Gene3D, SUPERFAMILY, and PROSITE hits were retained in the source records and contributed mapped InterPro entries; separate predictor blocks for each of these libraries were not fitted.

**9. UniProt mapping and complementary annotations.** CARD-associated protein accessions were mapped to UniProtKB through its REST API. Candidate entries were accepted only after exact amino-acid sequence agreement with the corresponding CARD protein. Multiple exact matches were resolved by prioritizing reviewed entries, then annotation score, then accession order. Mapping status and available annotation evidence were retained. Exact mappings were available for 4,060 proteins, including 272 reviewed representatives.

Binary context annotations included GO and EC terms, annotated feature types, subcellular locations, cofactors, and available reaction cross-references. A separate numerical block contained seven generic features: annotated active-site, binding-site, transmembrane-region, signal-region, and disulfide-bond counts, plus transmembrane and signal-region coverage fractions. Coverage fractions used the union of annotated intervals. Only these generic numerical features entered the UniProt classifier block; protein identifiers, names, GO/EC labels, and resistance descriptions were excluded from its predictors. Unmapped proteins retained missing UniProt values. In mapped proteins, a zero indicated that an annotation was not recorded, rather than experimentally verified functional absence.

**10. Sequence-relatedness groups for annotation analyses.** Sequence relationships were estimated with [MMseqs2](https://github.com/soedinglab/MMseqs2) version 18.8cc5c using all-against-all `easy-search`, a minimum sequence identity of 0.40, coverage threshold 0.80 with coverage mode 0, alignment mode 3, and sensitivity 7.5. The maximum number of returned matches was set to the cohort size. Connected components of the qualifying similarity graph defined 369 sequence groups. Connected-component membership allowed transitive relationships and did not imply that every pair of proteins within a group directly met the search thresholds. These groups were used for cluster-aware association statistics, whole-cluster bootstrapping, and annotation ablation validation. They were not retroactively substituted into the earlier SHAP-profile analysis.

**11. Descriptor–annotation association testing.** Associations were tested between raw descriptor measurements and binary annotation membership. Eligible comparisons required at least ten annotated and ten unannotated proteins with finite descriptor values and a variable descriptor. Distributions were compared using two-sided asymptotic Mann–Whitney U tests. Effect magnitude and direction were summarized by Cliff's delta, calculated as \(2U/(n_1n_0)-1\), where positive values indicated larger measurements among annotated proteins. Group sizes, medians, means, standard deviations, missingness, and contributing sequence-group counts were retained.

Length-adjusted rank associations were calculated by ranking the descriptor and annotation variables, regressing each ranked variable on an intercept and standardized log protein length, and correlating the residuals. An accompanying residual regression used a sequence-cluster sandwich variance estimate, a finite-sample correction, and a t reference distribution with the number of contributing clusters minus one degrees of freedom. Whole-protein and segment-length descriptors were excluded from length-adjusted tests. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment was applied separately to the Mann–Whitney and cluster-adjusted P-value families within each analysis panel. Candidate pairs required both adjusted values below 0.05, an absolute Cliff's delta of at least 0.33, and at least five contributing clusters in each annotation state.

For screened global candidates, 2,000 whole-cluster bootstrap samples were drawn with replacement. Every selected cluster contributed all of its proteins, and ranks and length adjustment were recalculated in each sample. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of finite bootstrap correlations formed the interval; at least 90% of replicates had to yield valid estimates. Candidates whose intervals excluded zero were labelled stable exploratory associations. Because candidates were selected and assessed in the same dataset, these intervals were interpreted as post-selection stability measures rather than independent confirmatory evidence.

Conditional analyses correlated descriptors with quantitative Pfam scores, coverage measurements, and copy counts only among proteins carrying the corresponding domain, requiring at least 20 valid observations and variation in both variables. Ordinary and length-adjusted rank correlations were exported with multiple-testing corrections. Additional screens were performed within supported families, drug classes, and resistance mechanisms, and among reviewed exact UniProt mappings. Each stratum formed a separate multiple-testing panel; these stratified screens were not independently bootstrap-confirmed. The five-cluster-per-state criterion belonged to the binary candidate screen and should not be assumed to apply to every conditional correlation.

**12. Matched annotation ablations and shuffled-label controls.** Five predictor configurations were compared using the same eligible targets and outer folds within each cohort: physicochemical descriptors alone (R1); Pfam presence, counts, quantitative measurements, and InterPro presence (R2); the seven generic UniProt measurements (R3); combined annotation features (R4 = R2 + R3); and all features combined (R5 = R1 + R2 + R3). Separate experiments used the complete cohort, exact UniProt-mapped proteins, and reviewed UniProt-mapped proteins. Targets required at least ten observations in each state and sufficient sequence-group support for outer and inner validation.

Outer folds were selected from a maximum of five while maintaining sequence-group separation and target-state support. The saved complete-cohort and exact-mapping experiments used two outer folds, while the reviewed subset used five. The complete-cohort comparison evaluated ten family and 24 drug-class targets. Thresholds were selected within up to three grouped inner folds by maximizing F1 over 0.05–0.95 in increments of 0.05, with ties favouring thresholds nearest 0.50. Targets without valid inner grouped splits were excluded. Predictor filtering and imputation were fitted separately inside each training partition, and the fixed LightGBM settings described above were used.

Evaluation included AP, AUROC, F1, precision, recall, confusion-matrix counts, Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC), and normalized MCC, defined as \((\mathrm{MCC}+1)/2\). Changes from R1 were calculated for each matched target within each cohort. A single joint row permutation of the multilabel outcome matrix supplied the shuffled-label control for each cohort and was shared across feature configurations. This preserved label co-occurrence within rows while breaking the original sequence–label correspondence. The control was a diagnostic comparison; one shuffle did not provide a permutation P-value. Comparisons across different cohorts or against the earlier descriptor-grouped classifiers were not treated as matched ablations.

**13. Integrating SHAP importance with biological annotations.** Target-specific SHAP-selected descriptors were joined to stable global descriptor–annotation associations by feature name. Annotation prevalence within each target and among other cohort proteins was then attached using target type, target label, and annotation identifier. For UniProt terms, prevalence denominators included only exact-mapped proteins. The resulting `SHAP_feature_domain_context.csv` connected predictive importance, descriptor–annotation effect estimates, cluster-adjusted significance, bootstrap intervals, and target annotation prevalence.

Family-level interpretation required checking that the annotation actually occurred in the target proteins; the feature-based join alone did not establish that connection. In particular, rows with missing target prevalence could arise when a globally associated annotation was absent from a target and did not support a target-specific domain explanation. Higher prevalence than the background provided descriptive context rather than an independently tested enrichment result. Mean absolute SHAP supplied feature importance, whereas raw group summaries and adjusted correlations supplied association direction. Global associations were not presented as demonstrated within-family effects without supporting stratified evidence. Related Pfam and InterPro terms were recognized as overlapping evidence, and annotations computed from the same sequence were not treated as independent experimental validation. Whole-protein compositional associations could motivate mechanistic hypotheses but could not localize a change to a catalytic site or establish a causal resistance mechanism.

**14. Computational reproducibility.** Input hashes, sequence manifests, feature schemas, fold assignments, target exclusions, model configurations, annotation provenance, and cached predictions were retained. Recorded versions included Pfeature 1.4, Biopython 1.86, NumPy 2.4.4, pandas 2.3.3, SciPy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.8.0, LightGBM 4.7.0, and SHAP 0.52.0. Seed 0 was specified for the principal reproducible operations; bootstrap seeds were derived deterministically from analysis fingerprints. The companion evidence file identifies source notebooks, configuration records, output tables, and paper sections supporting this draft.
