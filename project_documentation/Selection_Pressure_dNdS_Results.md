# Selection pressure (dN/dS) in AMR gene families of ESKAPE pathogens

## Summary

Selection pressure was estimated for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes detected in the six ESKAPE pathogens by comparing each gene with a reference sequence from its own gene family. Of 19,071 genes in 92 gene families, 407 genes (2.1%) in 24 families had a ratio of non-synonymous to synonymous substitution rates (dN/dS) greater than 1, the expected signature of positive (diversifying) selection. These genes fall into seven classes: efflux pumps (105 genes), glycopeptide resistance genes (85), the pmr phosphoethanolamine transferase (84), β-lactamases (67), aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes (40), macrolide phosphotransferases (18), and a small group of other resistance genes (8). The largest numbers were found in *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (156 genes) and *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (134 genes).

## What was analysed

- **Genes:** AMR genes identified in *Acinetobacter baumannii*, *Enterococcus faecium*, *Enterobacter* spp., *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Staphylococcus aureus*, grouped by AMR gene family. Each of the 92 families analysed contained between 10 and 3,701 genes.
- **Comparison:** within each family, every gene was compared with a single reference sequence of that family. dN, dS and dN/dS were estimated by maximum likelihood with PAML; all 19,071 comparisons completed.
- **Criterion for positive selection:** dN/dS > 1 with a measurable synonymous rate (dS > 0).

## Gene-family classes under positive selection

**Efflux pumps (105 genes, 2 families).** The small multidrug resistance (SMR) efflux pump family had 83 positively selected genes out of 567 (15%), the highest proportion among the four families with more than 500 genes. Most were in *K. pneumoniae* (49 of 171) and *S. aureus* (26 of 66). The major facilitator superfamily (MFS) efflux pumps had 22 positively selected genes out of 3,701 (0.6%). Because these pumps act on many drug classes, the result is consistent with diversification of multidrug efflux systems.

**Glycopeptide resistance (85 genes, 2 families).** Positively selected genes were found in the glycopeptide resistance gene cluster in *P. aeruginosa* (57 of 301), *E. faecium* (22 of 241) and *S. aureus* (4 of 137), and in vanW in *E. faecium* (2 of 17). This is consistent with diversification of genes associated with resistance to glycopeptide antibiotics.

**Lipid A modification, pmr phosphoethanolamine transferase (84 genes, 1 family).** All 84 genes were in *P. aeruginosa* (84 of 1,054). This family confers resistance to peptide antibiotics, the class that includes the polymyxins. The result is consistent with positive selection acting on this resistance mechanism in *P. aeruginosa*.

**β-lactamases and β-lactam targets (72 genes, 10 families).** Nine β-lactamase families contained positively selected genes (67 genes). Most were TEM (25 genes; 21 of 47 in *K. pneumoniae*) and SHV (27 of 285, all in *K. pneumoniae*). The remainder were in CARB, KPC, OXA, OXA-1-like, OXA-23-like, VEB and BlaZ. In addition, 5 of 94 methicillin-resistant PBP2 genes in *S. aureus* met the criterion. Together these cover penicillins, cephalosporins, monobactams and carbapenems, consistent with ongoing diversification of β-lactam resistance determinants.

**Aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes (40 genes, 6 families).** Positively selected genes were found in acetyltransferases (AAC(3)), nucleotidyltransferases (ANT(2'')), phosphotransferases (APH(3'), APH(3''), APH(6)) and the aminoglycoside bifunctional resistance protein. They were present in all six pathogens. The highest proportions were in APH(3') in *S. aureus* (12 of 12) and *E. faecium* (6 of 7). This is consistent with diversification of enzymes that inactivate aminoglycosides.

**Macrolide phosphotransferases, MPH (18 genes, 1 family).** Positively selected genes were found in *K. pneumoniae* (12 of 33), *Enterobacter* spp. (5 of 11) and *P. aeruginosa* (1 of 6).

**Other (3 genes, 2 families).** Trimethoprim-resistant dihydrofolate reductase dfr (one gene each in *Enterobacter* spp. and *K. pneumoniae*) and rifampin ADP-ribosyltransferase Arr (one gene in *K. pneumoniae*).

## Notes

1. **Comparisons not counted.** 215 comparisons had dS = 0, meaning the gene differed from the reference only at non-synonymous sites, so dN/dS cannot be calculated as a finite value. A further 74 genes were identical to the reference (dN = dS = 0). Neither group is counted as positively selected. In 20 families, the only dN/dS > 1 values came from dS = 0 comparisons, so these families do not appear in Table 1: ANT(4'); ANT(9); CMY, GES, NDM, OXA-50-like, OXA-51-like and PER beta-lactamases; General Bacterial Porin with reduced permeability to peptide antibiotics; ampR transcriptional regulator; antibiotic-resistant GlpT, isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase (ileS) and murA transferase; daptomycin resistant mprF; fluoroquinolone resistant gyrA, gyrB, parC and parE; vanH; vanR.
2. **Drug class.** Drug classes are those assigned by CARD/RGI to each gene family across the ESKAPE genomes.
3. **Efflux pump drug classes.** MFS antibiotic efflux pump: aminoglycoside antibiotic; bicyclomycin-like antibiotic; carbapenem; cephalosporin; diaminopyrimidine antibiotic; disinfecting agents and antiseptics; fluoroquinolone antibiotic; glycylcycline; macrolide antibiotic; moenomycin antibiotic; monobactam; nucleoside antibiotic; oxazolidinone antibiotic; penicillin beta-lactam; peptide antibiotic; phenicol antibiotic; phosphonic acid antibiotic; rifamycin antibiotic; tetracycline antibiotic. SMR antibiotic efflux pump: aminocoumarin antibiotic; aminoglycoside antibiotic; cephalosporin; disinfecting agents and antiseptics; macrolide antibiotic; peptide antibiotic; rifamycin antibiotic; tetracycline antibiotic.
4. **Unnamed family.** One family (ID f142, 818 *P. aeruginosa* genes including MexA, MexB, MexR, MexG, nalC, nalD and ArmR) carries the label "nan" in the family ID mapping file. None of its genes had dN/dS > 1.
